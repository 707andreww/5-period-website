What is sepak takraw?

Sepak takraw is a net sport where two teams kick a woven ball back and forth over a net, volleyball-style, but you can't use your hands or arms. Feet, knees, chest, and head only. The name mixes Malay (sepak, "kick") and Thai (takraw, "woven ball").

It's huge in Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Myanmar, Laos, and the Philippines, and it's played at the Asian Games and SEA Games. The signature move is the spike: a player flips upside down and smashes the ball over the net with their foot.

How to play

If you know volleyball, you already know 70% of this. Swap hands for feet and you're most of the way there.

  1. Set up the court

    The court is 13.4 m × 6.1 m (44 × 20 ft), the same size as a doubles badminton court, so any badminton court works. The net sits at about 1.52 m for men and 1.42 m for women. Each half has a service circle (30 cm radius) near the back.

  2. Serve it up

    The server (the tekong) stands with one foot in the service circle. A teammate at the net tosses the ball to them by hand, and the tekong kicks it over the net in one touch. The ball can't touch the net on the way over in most rule sets, and the tekong's planted foot has to stay in the circle until the kick.

  3. Three touches, no hands

    Each side gets up to 3 touches to get the ball back over. Unlike volleyball, the same player can touch it more than once, even all three times. Any body part is legal except hands and arms (below the shoulder).

  4. Set and spike

    The classic pattern: receive with a kick or chest, set the ball high near the net, then the striker attacks with a roll spike (bicycle kick) or sunback spike (sole of the foot over the net). Headers count too.

  5. Score points

    It's rally scoring: every rally wins somebody a point. You win a point when the ball lands in on their side, or when they mess up: ball out, more than 3 touches, touching the net, crossing into the other half, or hitting it with a hand or arm.

  6. Win the match

    Matches are usually best of 3 sets. A set goes to 21 points, win by 2 (capped at 25). If it goes to a third set, that one's usually played to 15. Exact scoring and serve-rotation rules shift a bit between competitions and federation updates, so check the rules for your league.

Beginner tips

  • Start with juggling. Keep the ball up with the inside of your foot (the "inside kick"). It's the bread-and-butter touch.
  • Stretch your hips and hamstrings. Seriously. Spiking is basically a gymnastics move.
  • Learn the sunback spike before the roll spike. It's way easier on your body and you'll land on your feet.
  • Use a practice ball first. Competition balls are hard. A softer one saves your feet while you learn.

Team size & positions

A sepak takraw team is called a regu. The standard format is 3 on 3.

Standard

Regu

3 v 3

3 players on court, plus up to 2 subs, so a roster of 5. This is what you'll see at the Asian Games.

Smaller

Doubles

2 v 2

2 players on court plus a sub. Faster, way more running, popular for beach and casual play.

Bigger

Quadrant

4 v 4

4 players on court plus 1 sub. Newer format with longer rallies and more defense.

Tournament

Team event

3 regus

A "team" is three separate regus, about 12–15 players total. Win 2 of the 3 regu matches to win the tie.

Positions in a regu

Tekong (server)

Plays at the back. Serves every point and covers deep defense. Needs a powerful, accurate kick.

Feeder (setter)

Front of the court, one side of the net. Tosses the ball for the serve and sets up the spiker. Needs soft, precise touch.

Striker (killer)

Front of the court, other side. Spikes and blocks. Usually the most acrobatic player on the team.

Equipment & prices

Good news: this is a cheap sport. Prices below are typical US retail ranges in USD and move around a lot by brand and where you buy (they're often much cheaper in Southeast Asia).

⚽Required

Takraw ball

Made from woven synthetic plastic (traditionally rattan) with 12 holes. Men's balls weigh about 170–180 g, women's 150–160 g. Marathon is the big official brand.

Shoes
