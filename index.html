02 / The sport

History and organizations

  • 1400s / Sepak ragaThe Malay Annals describe a rattan-ball kicking game in the Malacca Sultanate. Players kept the ball up in a circle, with no net.
  • 1945 / Over the netHamid Mydin introduced a net version in Penang, Malaya, played on a badminton court.
  • 1960 / One name, one rulebookDelegates from Malaya, Singapore, Myanmar, and Thailand met in Kuala Lumpur and agreed on the name "sepak takraw."
  • 1990 / Asian GamesSepak takraw became an Asian Games medal sport in Beijing.

Name: Malay sepak ("kick") + Thai takraw ("woven ball").
ASTAF: Asian Sepaktakraw Federation, formed 1965.
ISTAF: International Sepaktakraw Federation, formed 1988. It runs world events and the rulebook.

Sources: Sepak takraw (Wikipedia), ISTAF (Wikipedia).

03 / Getting set up

Equipment, cost, and facilities

  • Equipment: a takraw ball, a net, and flat-soled sneakers. Share one ball per regu (team of three).
  • Facility: a gym with badminton courts. A badminton court is the same 13.4 m × 6.1 m size, and a regulation badminton net already sits at men's takraw height.
  • DIY: no courts? Tape a 6.1 m-wide court and string a rope at about 1.5 m. A light volleyball or beach ball works for practice.
  • Check first: tighten nets, clear the walls behind each court, and confirm everyone has closed-toe shoes.

04 / How it works

Court and kicking skills

  • Goal: make the ball land on the other side. Each side gets up to 3 touches. The same player may touch it more than once.
  • Inside kick: the main touch. Lift the ball with the inside of the foot, knee bent out, toes up.
  • Other legal touches: instep, sole, knee, chest, and head. Never hands or arms.
  • Positions: the tekong serves from the circle. The feeder sets, and the striker attacks at the net.
Official measurements and scoring

Net: 1.52 m at center for men, 1.42 m for women. Ball: 42–44 cm around, 170–180 g (men's).
Service circle: 30 cm radius, 2.45 m from the back line.

Scoring: rally point. Best of 3 sets to 21, win by 2, capped at 25.
Serve: a teammate tosses by hand, and the tekong kicks it over. Teams alternate serving every three points.

Official ISTAF-style measurements. Class courts and nets can be adapted on slide 5.

05 / Playing in class

Rules, safety, and modifications

  • Class rulesThree touches per side, no hands or arms. Serve with a hand toss from a teammate. Don't touch the net or cross under it. Rally scoring.
  • ScoringClass games go to 7, win by 1. A ball that lands in counts, as does any fault by the other side. Rotate the server every point so everyone serves.
  • SafetyNo bicycle kicks, roll spikes, or jumping attacks in class. Feet stay below shoulder height near others. Wait 2 m back from active courts. Warm up hips and hamstrings, and stop on "Freeze."
  • ModificationsUse a light volleyball or beach ball. Allow one catch per side, or one bounce between touches. Lower the net or shorten the court. Add a seated or hands-allowed role. Remove the catch for more challenge.

These are classroom rules and adaptations. Official play uses sets to 21. Agree on each regu's modifications before scoring.

06 / 39 students, 52 minutes

One-day lesson

  1. 0–8 minIntro and video

    Goal and rules, then ~2 minutes of the rules video.

  2. 8–13 minWarm-up

    Jog 2 min, leg swings 1 min, hip circles 1 min, hamstring stretch 1 min.

  3. 13–18 minTeacher demonstration

    Safe area, inside kick, knee touch, serve toss, scoring, and Freeze.

  4. 18–28 minSkills / drills

    Self toss-and-kick, then regu keep-up circles. Five observed inside kicks each.

  5. 28–36 minModified play

    Catch allowed, lowered net, feedback without competition.

  6. 36–46 minGame

    Regu vs. regu games to 7. Rotate courts and the keep-up station.

  7. 46–52 minQuiz and closure

    Quiz 4 min, then a cue, a safety rule, and cleanup.

Lesson details and teacher demonstration

Objectives: perform a controlled inside kick (ball rises to about head height and stays catchable) in 3 of 5 attempts. Keep score accurately and follow the safety rules.

Organization: 13 regus of three. Rotate tekong, feeder, and striker each game. Use 4 badminton courts (8 regus playing) plus a keep-up station along the wall (5 regus). Rotate after every game.

Drill: each student self-tosses and kicks with the inside of the foot, then catches, 10 times. Next, each regu keeps the ball up in a circle (one catch allowed) and counts touches. Then observe five inside kicks per student.

Game: modified play first (catch allowed, lower net), then remove the catch if a regu is ready. Games to 7, win by 1.

Teacher: set a 2 m waiting zone behind each court. Demonstrate the inside kick: knee out, toes up, contact under the ball, eyes on it. Show a knee touch and a header. Model the serve toss to the tekong, a point being scored, and Freeze. Ask students to repeat the cues, then coach one correction at a time.
